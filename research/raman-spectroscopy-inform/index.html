Machine learning can make Raman checks of edible oils faster

  • Paper published April 2020
  • Summary updated November 27, 2026
  • 2 minute read

This article in INFORM, the AOCS magazine, explains how machine learning can speed up Raman spectroscopy for edible oils. I am one of six authors, from my time as a research assistant at the University of Nebraska-Lincoln.

Read the paper at the publisher

The problem: a useful test that is slow to read

Raman spectroscopy can test oils without damaging them. It is used to describe the chemistry of fats, tell oils apart, and detect adulteration and oxidation. The authors set out to make that analysis more efficient.

How it works: machine learning reads the spectra

The authors used edible oils as the test case. They integrated machine learning into Raman spectroscopy so that it could pick out spectral features and detect oil type, adulteration, and oxidation quickly and accurately.

Who it serves: readers of the AOCS magazine

The article was written for INFORM readers. The same research group later published the full peer-reviewed study on oil type and adulteration in Food Chemistry.
