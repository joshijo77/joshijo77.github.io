One egg white protein could replace gluten and xanthan gum in veggie sausage

  • Paper published October 2022
  • Summary updated November 27, 2026
  • 2 minute read

This open access paper in Food Bioengineering tested egg white proteins as a replacement for gluten and xanthan gum in plant-based sausage. I am one of nine authors. As a research assistant, I worked on reformulating a gluten-free veggie sausage to compare with a beef sausage.

Read the paper at the publisher

The problem: common binders do not suit everyone

Plant-based sausages often use gluten or xanthan gum for texture. Those ingredients do not suit people with gluten sensitivity or shoppers who want clean labels.

How it works: test two egg white proteins

The team tested two commercial egg white proteins, P110 and M200, at levels from 0% to 10%. They measured color, texture, length, and weight loss, and used Raman spectroscopy to study the protein gels.

What it found: M200 came closest to meat

  • M200 gave the texture closest to a commercial meat sausage.
  • For texture, 5% M200 ranked first, then 5% P110, 3% gluten, and 1.3% xanthan gum.
  • The egg white proteins had no significant effect on color, length, or weight loss.
  • The M200 gel showed more alpha helix structure and a more ordered arrangement than the P110 gel.

Who it serves: makers of plant-based meat

The authors conclude that M200 could replace both gluten and xanthan gum in veggie sausage. They add that the approach may apply to other meat analogs.
