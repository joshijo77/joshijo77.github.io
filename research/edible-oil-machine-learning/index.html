Published research · Food Chemistry · 2022

Machine learning identified edible oil type with 96.7% accuracy

  • Paper published March 2022
  • Summary updated November 27, 2026
  • 2 minute read

This paper in Food Chemistry paired Raman spectroscopy with nine supervised machine learning algorithms to check edible oils quickly. I am one of seven authors, from my time as a research assistant at the University of Nebraska-Lincoln.

Read the paper at the publisherFree author manuscript on PubMed Central

The problem: oil checks are slow and use chemicals

Labs usually confirm an oil type by measuring its fatty acids with gas chromatography. That method needs a chemical step first, which takes time and produces toxic solvent waste.

Raman spectroscopy needs no chemical reagents. Its spectral analysis, however, has been slow and low throughput, which has limited its use.

How it works: spectra in, answers out

The team collected Raman spectra for ten commercial edible oils from a variety of brands. Nine supervised machine learning algorithms learned to read those spectra. The results were then compared with a principal component analysis model.

What it found: high accuracy without chemical preparation

  • The best model identified oil type with 96.7% accuracy.
  • It predicted adulteration with an R² of 0.984.
  • Several algorithms beat principal component analysis at sorting oils by fatty acid profile, reaching 100% accuracy.

Who it serves: labs that need fast food analysis

The authors offer the method as a model for rapid food analysis that pairs conventional Raman spectroscopy or gas chromatography with machine learning.
